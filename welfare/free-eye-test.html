Free eye test and glasses with PRSI

Your PRSI pays for a free eye test every two years and towards glasses or contact lenses. You just need an optician on the Department's panel.

gov.ie · Checked November 2026

Key facts

  • Free eye test once every second calendar year.
  • Glasses: one pair (reading, distance, bifocal or varifocal) every second calendar year. Basic frames are free; €42 towards dearer frames.
  • Or contact lenses, including disposables – for sight, not cosmetic reasons.
  • Not covered: driving licence and computer-screen (VDU) eye tests.
  • Medical contact lenses: up to €500 per lens with an ophthalmologist's recommendation.

Do you qualify?

  • You need PRSI class A, E, P, H or S contributions – most employees (class A) and the self-employed (class S).
  • Under 21: at least 39 paid contributions at any time.
  • Aged 21–28: 39 paid contributions, plus 39 paid or credited in the governing year (2025 for claims in 2027; 2024 for claims in 2026) – or 26 paid in each of the second and third last years.
  • Aged 29–65: at least 260 paid contributions (about 5 years' work), plus the same governing-year test.

How to claim

  • Book with an optician, dentist or hearing aid supplier that's on the Department's panel – ask when you book.
  • Give them your PPS number and date of birth. They check online that you qualify before treatment.
  • Sign the consent form. They claim the money from the Department – you only pay any balance.
  • No application form, no MyWelfare – the provider does it.

Questions

How often can I get a free eye test with PRSI?

Once every second calendar year – for example once in 2026 and again in 2028.

Are glasses free with PRSI?

Basic frames and lenses are free. If you choose more expensive frames, the Department pays €42 towards them and you pay the rest.

Can I get contact lenses instead of glasses?

Yes – one pair of glasses or contact lenses (including disposables) every second calendar year, for sight only, not cosmetic lenses.

Is a driving licence eye test covered?

No. Sight tests for driving licences or for computer screen (VDU) work aren't covered.

Can I claim tax relief on glasses?

Routine eye tests and glasses don't get health expenses tax relief. Use Treatment Benefit instead.
