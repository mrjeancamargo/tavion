Moving home: mortgages for second-time buyers

Trading up, trading down or moving for space? How much a second-time buyer can borrow, how your equity counts, and what to do if you're not selling first.

Checked November 2026

Key facts

  • Second and subsequent buyers can borrow up to 3.5× gross income (first-time buyers 4×), with at least a 10% deposit.
  • Your equity – what your home is worth minus your mortgage and selling costs – usually becomes your deposit.
  • Selling costs: estate agent fees (around 1–1.5% + VAT) and a solicitor for the sale.
  • From April 2026, short-term bridging loans (up to 18 months, repaid from the sale) are exempt from the income limit – useful for buying before you sell.
  • If you're in negative equity, you're exempt from the deposit (LTV) limit but not the income limit.

Example: trading up

A couple earning €70,000 and €50,000 with a home worth €350,000 and €150,000 left on the mortgage have about €191,697 equity after selling costs. With €10,000 savings they can borrow up to €420,000 (3.5×) and buy up to about €610,000, repaying about €1,891 a month.

Selling first, buying first, or keeping your home?

OptionWhat to know
Sell firstClearest position for the lender; you may need to rent in between.
Buy first with a bridging loanExempt from the income limit since April 2026 if the term is 18 months or less and it's repaid from the sale. LTV still applies.
Keep your current homeYour existing mortgage counts against affordability; the new loan is still capped at 3.5× income. If you rent the old home out you'll need a buy-to-let mortgage (70% LTV).
Trading downYour equity may cover most of the price; you might not need a mortgage at all.

Tips for movers

  • Get a valuation from 2–3 agents before you decide what to buy
  • Ask your lender about porting your mortgage – and compare it with switching
  • Check if your fixed rate has an early repayment charge
  • Movers can't use Help to Buy, but some can use the First Home Scheme in limited cases
