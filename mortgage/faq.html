Mortgage FAQ

Answers to the questions people ask most about getting a mortgage in Ireland.

Checked November 2026

How much can I borrow for a mortgage in Ireland?

First-time buyers up to 4× gross income and second and subsequent buyers up to 3.5×. Couples combine their incomes. Lenders can go above this for a limited number of borrowers.

How much deposit do I need?

At least 10% of the price for a home you'll live in, and 30% for buy-to-let. You also need money for stamp duty and fees.

Does bonus or overtime count?

Lenders may count some of your regular bonus, overtime or commission – often around half of the average of the last 2 years, if it's guaranteed or consistent. It varies by lender.

Can I get a mortgage on my own?

Yes – a sole applicant can borrow up to 4× their income as a first-time buyer. Help to Buy and the First Home Scheme help close the gap on new homes.

Does my partner need to be on the mortgage?

Only if you want their income counted. Joint applicants are both responsible for the full loan.

What is a second and subsequent buyer?

Anyone who has owned a home before. The limit is 3.5× income instead of 4×. Some people who've been through divorce or insolvency may be treated as first-time buyers – ask your lender.

What happens to my equity when I move?

Your equity (home value minus mortgage and selling costs) usually becomes your deposit for the next home.

Can I buy before I sell?

Yes, with a bridging loan. Since April 2026, bridging loans of up to 18 months repaid from the sale are exempt from the income limit, but the 90% LTV limit still applies.

How much is stamp duty?

1% of the price up to €1 million, 2% between €1m and €1.5m, and 6% above €1.5m. On new homes it's charged on the price excluding VAT.

What is the average mortgage rate in Ireland?

3.53% for new mortgages in August 2026 (fixed rates averaged 3.49%), according to the Central Bank.

What are the repayments on €300,000?

About €1,352 a month over 30 years at 3.53%. See every rate and term.

How long can a mortgage be?

Up to 35 years with most lenders, usually ending by age 68–70.

Is it cheaper to buy than rent?

Often, month to month: the average new rent is €1,839 (RTB, Q1 2026). But buying has upfront costs, insurance and maintenance. Compare by county.

How much is Help to Buy now?

Up to €35,000 from 7 October 2026 (Budget 2027) for first-time buyers of new homes up to €500,000.

Do I need mortgage protection insurance?

Yes, lenders require it for a home loan (with a few exceptions, such as age or health). You also need home insurance.

What is approval in principle?

A lender's provisional offer based on your income and savings, usually valid for 6 months. You need it before going sale agreed.

Is this calculator a mortgage offer?

No. It's a free, independent estimate based on the Central Bank rules and published rates. Only a lender can approve a mortgage.
