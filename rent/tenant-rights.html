Tenant rights in Ireland

Your rights as a tenant in Ireland – rent increases, notices to leave, deposits and where to get help. Updated for the 1 March 2026 rules.

Checked November 2026

Key facts

  • Your rent can only go up once every 12 months, by 2% a year at most (or CPI if lower), with 90 days' written notice that is also sent to the RTB.
  • After 6 months you have security of tenure – your landlord needs a legal reason to end the tenancy.
  • A notice to leave must be in writing, signed, dated, give the reason and a termination date with the full notice period – otherwise you can challenge it.
  • You have 90 days (28 for breach cases) to refer a notice dispute to the RTB.
  • Deposits: a landlord can ask for at most 1 month's rent as deposit and 1 month in advance.

Tips: what to do when you get a rent increase

  1. Check the dates. Was the rent last set at least 12 months ago (24 for the first review in an area designated on 20 June 2025)? Are you getting 90 days' notice?
  2. Check the amount with our rent calculator and the official RTB Rent Calculator.
  3. Check the notice includes the RTB Rent Calculator printout and 3 comparable rents from the RTB Rent Register, and that it was sent to the RTB.
  4. Dispute it with the RTB if it's too high or invalid – before the new rent starts, or within 28 days of getting the notice, whichever is later.

Tips: what to do when you get a Notice of Termination

  1. Don't panic and don't leave early – you can stay until the termination date, and an invalid notice doesn't end the tenancy.
  2. Check it with the notice period calculator: right reason for your tenancy and landlord size? Long enough notice? Sent to the RTB? Statutory declaration attached?
  3. Ask the RTB to confirm whether your landlord has 3 or fewer tenancies if they're relying on a small-landlord reason.
  4. Get free advice from Threshold (1800 454 454) and refer a dispute to the RTB within the deadline if you think the notice is invalid.
  5. If the reason doesn't happen – no sale, nobody moves in – the landlord may have to offer you the tenancy back. Tell the RTB.

Your rights in a rented home

  • A rent book or written record of rent paid, and a copy of the tenancy agreement
  • Privacy – the landlord can only enter with your permission, except in an emergency
  • Repairs – the landlord must keep the property to minimum standards (heating, damp, safety)
  • Your deposit back promptly at the end, minus only unpaid rent or damage beyond normal wear and tear
  • No penalisation for contacting the RTB or asserting your rights
  • Your tenancy must be registered with the RTB – you can check the public register

Your responsibilities

  • Pay rent on time
  • Don't damage the property or cause anti-social behaviour
  • Let the landlord in for repairs and inspections (by arrangement)
  • Give the right notice when you leave
