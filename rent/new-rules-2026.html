New rental rules from 1 March 2026

On 1 March 2026 Ireland's rental rules changed. Here's what's different for tenants and landlords – and what stays the same for existing tenancies.

Checked against RTB guidance · November 2026

Key facts

  • The new rules apply to tenancies created on or after 1 March 2026. Existing tenancies keep the old termination rules.
  • Rent can rise by 2% a year, or CPI inflation if lower, once every 12 months – for all private tenancies, old and new.
  • New tenancies have a 6-year Tenancy of Minimum Duration. Landlords with 4+ tenancies can only end them for a tenant breach or if the property no longer suits.
  • Rent can be reset to market rent only at the start of a new tenancy (not after a no-fault eviction) and at the end of each 6-year cycle.
  • Every rent notice and Notice of Termination must be sent to the RTB on the same day as the tenant, or it's invalid.

When can rent be reset to market rent at the start of a new tenancy?

Only if the previous tenancy ended because the tenant left by choice, the tenant breached their obligations, or the property no longer suited the tenant's needs. Also for a first-ever letting, a home with no tenancy for 2 years (1 year for a protected structure), or after a substantial renovation where the home was offered back to the previous tenant. Not if the landlord ended the last tenancy in the previous 2 years to sell, move in a family member or change the use (a "no-fault termination") – including in the first 6 months.

The landlord must give a statement that the rent is not above market rent, backed by 3 comparable tenancies from the RTB Rent Register that began in the last 3 months.

Other changes

  • Sales with tenants in place: the new owner steps into the landlord's shoes and the tenant keeps the rest of their cycle.
  • Landlord size counts on the day notice is served. A small landlord must include a statement confirming they have 1–3 tenancies. Tenants can ask the RTB to confirm.
  • Longer time to challenge a notice: 90 days to refer a dispute to the RTB where there's no breach (28 days for breaches).
  • RTB Rent Register: new rents must be justified with 3 comparable rents.

Questions

Do the March 2026 rental rules apply to my existing tenancy?

The new termination rules (6-year minimum duration, limits for large landlords) only apply to tenancies created from 1 March 2026. The rent cap (2% or CPI if lower) applies to all private tenancies, and existing tenancies can't be reset to market rent.

What is a Tenancy of Minimum Duration?

From 1 March 2026, new tenancies run in rolling 6-year cycles. After the first 6 months the tenant has security of tenure, and a small landlord can only end the tenancy for limited reasons during each cycle.

Who counts as a large landlord?

A landlord with 4 or more tenancies, or any company landlord. It's counted on the day the Notice of Termination is served.
