Rent and tenancy FAQ

Answers to common questions about rent increases, Rent Pressure Zones and ending a tenancy in Ireland – for tenants and landlords.

Checked November 2026

How much can a landlord increase rent in Ireland in 2026?

By at most 2% a year, or by inflation (CPI) if lower, worked out pro rata from when the rent was last set. Inflation was 4.1% in the 12 months to September 2026, so the 2% cap applies. Increases are allowed once every 12 months with 90 days' notice.

How often can rent be increased?

Once every 12 months. In areas that only became Rent Pressure Zones on 20 June 2025, the first review for an existing tenancy must wait 24 months from when the rent was last set.

Is my area a Rent Pressure Zone?

Yes – the whole of Ireland has been a Rent Pressure Zone since 20 June 2025. See which areas were RPZs before that date on our RPZ page.

If my rent hasn't gone up in years, can the landlord add 2% for every year?

The cap is 2% a year pro rata over the time since the rent was last set, or the CPI change over that period if lower. So a rent unchanged for 3 years could rise by up to about 6% if inflation over those years was at least that much. Use the RTB Rent Calculator for the exact figure.

Can my landlord reset my rent to market rent?

Not if your tenancy started before 1 March 2026. For tenancies from 1 March 2026, rent can be reset at the start of a new tenancy (unless the previous one ended with a no-fault termination) and at the end of each 6-year cycle.

Does the rent cap apply to new builds?

Apartments where construction started after 10 June 2025 can increase in line with CPI with no 2% cap. They're still limited to once every 12 months.

How much notice does my landlord have to give for a rent increase?

At least 90 days in writing, sent to you and the RTB on the same day. It must include the RTB Rent Calculator printout and 3 comparable rents from the RTB Rent Register.

How do I challenge a rent increase?

Refer a dispute to the RTB before the new rent takes effect, or within 28 days of receiving the notice, whichever is later. Keep paying your current rent in the meantime.

How much notice does a landlord need to give to end a tenancy?

90 days under 6 months, 152 days for 6–12 months, 180 days for 1–7 years, 196 days for 7–8 years and 224 days for 8+ years. Shorter for breaches (28 days) and serious anti-social behaviour (7 days).

Can a landlord evict me to sell the house?

If your tenancy started before 1 March 2026: yes, with a statutory declaration that they intend to sell within 9 months. If it started from 1 March 2026: a landlord with 4+ tenancies can't; a landlord with 1–3 tenancies can only during the 6-year cycle to avoid undue hardship, or at the end of the cycle.

Can a landlord evict me so a family member can move in?

For tenancies before 1 March 2026, yes (private landlords). For tenancies from 1 March 2026, only small landlords, and during the cycle only for the landlord, spouse/civil partner, parent or child.

What is a no-fault eviction?

When a landlord ends a tenancy to sell, move in a family member or change the use, rather than because of anything the tenant did. After a no-fault termination, the landlord can't reset the rent to market rent for the next tenancy.

Does a notice to leave have to be sent to the RTB?

Yes. From 1 March 2026 a landlord must send the Notice of Termination to the tenant and the RTB on the same day, or it's invalid.

Is there a winter eviction ban?

No. The temporary ban on no-fault evictions between November and March ended on 31 March 2023. Notices can be served at any time of year.

How much deposit can a landlord ask for?

At most the equivalent of 1 month's rent as a deposit, and at most 1 month's rent in advance.

What if my landlord has 4 or more properties?

For tenancies created from 1 March 2026 they're a large landlord: after 6 months they can only end your tenancy for a breach of your obligations or if the property no longer suits your needs. If they sell, the buyer takes over your tenancy.

Where can I get free help?

Threshold (1800 454 454) gives free advice to renters. The RTB handles disputes, rent calculator and registration. Citizens Information explains your rights.

Is this calculator official?

No. It's a free, independent tool from Tavion based on the RTB's published rules. For an exact figure use the official RTB Rent Calculator, and for legal advice speak to Threshold or a solicitor.
