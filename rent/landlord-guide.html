Landlord guide: rent and notices

A plain-English guide for landlords: setting and reviewing rent, ending a tenancy correctly and the documents you need under the 1 March 2026 rules.

Checked November 2026

Key facts

  • Register every tenancy with the RTB and update the rent on your registration after each review.
  • Review rent at most once every 12 months, by up to 2% a year (or CPI if lower), with at least 90 days' notice – sent to the tenant and the RTB on the same day.
  • From 1 March 2026 a Notice of Termination that isn't sent to the RTB on the same day is invalid.
  • Landlords with 4+ tenancies (or companies) can't end new tenancies (from 1 March 2026) to sell, refurbish or move family in.
  • Use the RTB templates – they include the statutory declarations and statements each reason needs.

Reviewing rent: 5 steps

  1. Check the rules for your tenancy (when it started, new build, SSA, RPZ date).
  2. Calculate the maximum with the RTB Rent Calculator (or ours for a quick check), and confirm the rent isn't above market rent with 3 comparables from the RTB Rent Register.
  3. Complete the RTB Notice of Rent Review template (Part D if you claim a substantial change exemption).
  4. Send it to the tenant and the RTB on the same day, at least 90 days before the new rent starts.
  5. Update the rent on your RTB tenancy registration.

Common mistakes that make a notice invalid

  • Not sending it to the RTB on the same day (from 1 March 2026)
  • Too short a notice period – count from the tenancy length on the day you serve it
  • Missing reason, signature, date of service or termination date
  • Missing statutory declaration or statement
  • Using a reason that isn't allowed for the tenancy or your landlord size
  • Serving a 28-day arrears notice without a valid warning notice first

Small landlord selling during a 6-year cycle

A landlord with 1–3 tenancies can end a tenancy created from 1 March 2026 during the cycle to sell only to avoid undue financial or other hardship – where you, your spouse or civil partner need the proceeds for a home to live in, to repay a debt in full (or pay at least 15% of the asking price within 9 months, including tax or Fair Deal debt), or under a personal insolvency arrangement. Reaching retirement age on its own isn't hardship.
