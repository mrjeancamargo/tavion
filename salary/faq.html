Tax and take-home pay FAQ

Answers to common questions about tax, USC, PRSI and take-home pay in Ireland.

Checked November 2026

How is take-home pay calculated in Ireland?

Your employer deducts three things from your gross pay: income tax (PAYE) at 20% and 40% minus your tax credits, the Universal Social Charge (USC), and PRSI. What's left is your net or take-home pay. For example, a single person on €40,000 takes home €33,572.18 a year in 2026.

What is the 40% tax threshold in Ireland?

€44,000 for a single person in 2026, rising to €46,500 in 2027. It's €53,000 (€55,500 in 2027) for a married couple with one income and €48,000 (€50,500) for a single parent.

What tax credits does everyone get?

A single PAYE employee gets the Personal credit (€2,000) and the Employee credit (€2,000) – €4,000 a year in 2026 and €4,250 in 2027. Self-employed people get the Earned Income credit instead of the Employee credit.

What is joint assessment?

Married couples and civil partners can be taxed together. You share a bigger 20% band (€53,000, plus up to €35,000 more if you both work) and both personal credits. It usually saves tax when one partner earns much more than the other. Choose 'Married' in the calculator's advanced options.

Do medical card holders pay less USC?

Yes. If you have a full medical card (not a GP visit card) and your income is €60,000 or less, you pay a maximum USC rate of 2%: 0.5% on the first €12,012 and 2% on the rest. The same applies if you're 70 or over. The medical card concession runs to the end of 2027.

Who doesn't pay USC?

Anyone whose total income for the year is €13,000 or less pays no USC. Social welfare payments are also exempt from USC.

Who doesn't pay PRSI?

Employees earning €352 a week or less pay no PRSI, and people aged 66 or over don't pay employee PRSI. Between €352 and €424 a week you get a PRSI credit that reduces what you pay.

Why does PRSI change in October?

The government is increasing PRSI by stages to fund pensions: 4.2% to 4.35% on 1 October 2026, and 4.35% to 4.5% on 1 October 2027. The calculator uses a weighted average for the whole year.

How does a pension contribution reduce my tax?

Contributions to an occupational pension or PRSA get tax relief at your highest rate (20% or 40%), within age-related limits (15% of earnings under 30 up to 40% at 60+, on earnings up to €115,000). They don't reduce USC or PRSI.

What is the Rent Tax Credit?

If you rent your home, you can claim 20% of the rent you pay, up to €1,000 a person (€2,000 for a jointly assessed couple) in 2026, rising to €1,150 (€2,300) in 2027. Claim it in Revenue's myAccount.

Why is my payslip different from the calculator?

Common reasons: emergency tax (Revenue doesn't have your details yet), starting the job mid-year, benefit-in-kind such as a company car or health insurance, salary sacrifice, overtime, or tax credits split differently between spouses. Check your tax credit certificate in Revenue's myAccount.

What is emergency tax?

If your employer doesn't have a Revenue Payroll Notification for you, you're taxed at 40% with no credits and higher USC. Register your job in Revenue's myAccount to fix it – overpaid tax is refunded through your next payslips.

What is the difference between gross and net pay?

Gross pay is your salary before deductions. Net pay (take-home pay) is what's paid into your bank account after income tax, USC, PRSI and things like pension contributions.
