Your public holiday rights

What you're entitled to on a bank holiday in Ireland – full-time, part-time, sick, or on leave.

Citizens Information · Organisation of Working Time Act 1997 · Checked November 2026

Key facts

  • Full-time employees are entitled to public holiday benefit straight away.
  • Part-time employees qualify if they worked 40 hours in the 5 weeks before the holiday (annual leave counts as time worked).
  • Your employer chooses one of: a paid day off on the day, an extra day of annual leave, an extra day's pay, or a paid day off within a month.
  • Ask at least 21 days before which one applies. If your employer doesn't answer 14 days before, you get the day itself off with pay.
  • Work the holiday? You get one of the other options – usually an extra day's pay or a day in lieu.
  • Part-timers who don't normally work that day get one-fifth of their weekly pay.
  • Complaints go to the Workplace Relations Commission within 6 months.

Sick, on leave or laid off

  • On sick leave: you're still entitled to the benefit (unless off more than 26 weeks, or 52 for an occupational injury).
  • On maternity, paternity, parent's, parental or adoptive leave: you keep your public holiday entitlement.
  • Laid off: entitled for the first 13 weeks of lay-off.
  • Leaving the job: if you finish in the week before a holiday and worked the previous 4 weeks, you get an extra day's pay.
