Irish citizenship: frequently asked questions

Answers to the questions people ask most about Irish citizenship by naturalisation.

Checked against ISD guidance: November 2026

How many days do I need to apply for Irish citizenship?

On the standard route you need 5 years (1,825 or 1,826 days) of reckonable residence in the 9 years before you apply. That must include 1 full year of continuous residence immediately before your application, and 4 years in total in the 8 years before that year. Our calculator uses 1,826 days to be safe.

What is reckonable residence?

It's time you lived in Ireland legally on a permission that counts for citizenship. Stamps 1, 1A, 1G, 1H, 3, 4 and 5 count. Stamps 2 and 2A (students) don't count, and neither does time as an international protection applicant or any period without a valid, registered permission. See all stamps explained.

Does time on a student visa (Stamp 2) count?

No. Stamp 2 and Stamp 2A are not reckonable. Time on Stamp 1G after you graduate does count.

How long can I be outside Ireland in the year before I apply?

Up to 70 days in total in the year before your application, plus up to 30 extra days if the Minister accepts there were exceptional circumstances (for example family, personal or health reasons, work, study or humanitarian work). This rule has applied since 31 July 2023. Days you travel count as days in Ireland – only full days abroad are absences.

Do absences in the earlier years count against me?

ISD's residency calculator counts the days covered by your permissions, but the law talks about residence in Ireland. If you spent long periods abroad, ISD asks you to give as much detail as possible in your application. Our calculator shows both figures so you can see the difference.

Can I apply after my IRP or permission has expired?

No – ISD expects you to apply while your current permission is valid, and to keep your permission registered with no gaps. Gaps in registration can make your application ineligible.

What if I had a gap between two permissions?

Days in a gap aren't reckonable. A gap inside the final year before you apply also breaks your continuous residence, so you would need to wait until you have a full unbroken year again. The calculator flags gaps for you.

I'm married to an Irish citizen. What are the rules?

You need to have been married to (or in a civil partnership with) an Irish citizen for at least 3 years, be living together when you apply and when citizenship is granted, and have lived on the island of Ireland for 3 of the last 5 years (1,095 or 1,096 days), including 1 continuous year immediately before you apply. You'll also need proof of residence for your spouse and a statutory declaration. Choose the spouse route in the calculator.

I'm a refugee. When can I apply?

Refugees can apply 5 years from the exact date international protection was granted. There's no certification fee for recognised refugees, and there are special rules for proving identity if you don't have a passport. See requirements for refugees.

I'm from the EU, EEA, UK or Switzerland. Do I use this calculator?

No. You don't have immigration stamps, so you prove your residence with documents instead. You must have held EU/EEA, UK or Swiss nationality for more than 5 years – otherwise you apply as a non-EU national. See requirements for EU, UK and Swiss nationals.

How much does Irish citizenship cost?

The application fee is €175. If you're approved, the certification fee is €950 for adults, €200 for minors and for a widow, widower or surviving civil partner of an Irish citizen, and €0 for recognised refugees and stateless people.

What documents do I need?

Proof of identity worth 150 points (a certified colour copy of your passport's photo page is worth 150 on its own), and proof of residence worth 150 points for each year you claim – usually one Type A document (100 points) and one Type B document (50 points) per year. Copies must be certified. See documents and the scorecard.

How long does the application take?

ISD says most applications are processed within 12 months. Some take longer. Use our application tracker to see where you are.

Is there a citizenship test or English test?

No. Ireland does not currently have a citizenship knowledge test or an English language test for naturalisation. You must be of good character, intend to keep living in Ireland, and attend a citizenship ceremony.

Do I have to attend a citizenship ceremony?

Yes. It's mandatory for successful applicants aged 18 and over. You make a declaration of fidelity to the nation and loyalty to the State, and your certificate of naturalisation arrives by post in the weeks after. Children don't attend.

When can I get an Irish passport?

Once you have received your certificate of naturalisation by post, you can apply for an Irish passport.

Can my application be refused?

Yes. The Minister for Justice has absolute discretion. If refused, you're given a reason, but there is no appeal – you can apply again. Common reasons for an application failing include not reaching 150 points on the scorecard without an explanatory affidavit, incomplete forms and poor-quality documents. You lose the application fee.

Does Stamp 1G count towards citizenship?

Yes. ISD confirms that Stamp 1G counts, both for graduates and for spouses or partners of Critical Skills permit holders and researchers. See Stamp 1G explained.

Does Stamp 3 count towards citizenship?

Yes. Even though you can't work on Stamp 3, ISD confirms the time counts. See Stamp 3 explained.

Can I apply for my children?

A minor can't apply themselves – a parent or guardian applies for them. A naturalised parent can apply for their child; the Minister may also grant citizenship to children of Irish descent or association; and children born in Ireland on or after 1 January 2005 who weren't citizens at birth can apply after 1 year of continuous residence plus 2 years in the 8 years before that. See applying for a child.

What is a dependant young adult application?

If you're aged 18–23, came to Ireland legally as part of a family, are in secondary school here (or went straight from secondary school into third level here) and still depend on your parents, you can apply as a dependant young adult. You still need to meet the adult residence requirements.

Is this calculator official?

No. It's an independent, free tool from Tavion that follows the same counting method as ISD's own residency calculator, plus the absence rules for the final year. Your official reckonable residence is assessed by ISD when you apply.
