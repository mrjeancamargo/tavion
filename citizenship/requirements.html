Irish citizenship requirements

Everything you need to apply for Irish citizenship by naturalisation: who's eligible, the residence rules for each route, documents, fees and what happens after you apply.

Checked against ISD guidance: November 2026

On this page: 5-year residence · Spouse of an Irish citizen · Refugees · Children · Young adults · EU/UK/Swiss · All conditions · Documents · Fees · How to apply · After you apply · If refused

1. Adults who have lived in Ireland for 5 years

  • You must be 18 or over and able to prove your identity
  • 1 year of continuous residence in Ireland immediately before you apply – you can be away for up to 70 days in that year, plus up to 30 more in exceptional circumstances at the Minister's discretion
  • In the 8 years before that year, a total of 4 years of residence
  • Non-EU/EEA/Swiss nationals: 1,825 or 1,826 days of reckonable residence based on your permission stamps, including 365 or 366 days of continuous residence immediately before you apply
  • Proof of residence for every year you claim
  • You must intend to keep living in Ireland, be willing to attend a citizenship ceremony and make a declaration of fidelity, and be of good character
If you had permission to live here but spent long periods abroad, ISD asks you to give as much detail as possible in your application.

2. Married to, or civil partner of, an Irish citizen

  • 18 or over and able to prove your identity
  • Married or in a civil partnership with an Irish citizen for 3 years or more – the relationship must be genuine and lasting
  • Living with your spouse or civil partner on the date you apply and on the date citizenship is granted
  • Lived legally on the island of Ireland for 3 of the last 5 years (1,095 or 1,096 reckonable days), including 1 full year of continuous residence immediately before you apply – again with up to 70 days abroad (+30 exceptional)
  • Proof of residence for every year – plus proof of residence for your Irish spouse or civil partner
  • A statutory declaration signed before a solicitor, Commissioner for Oaths, Peace Commissioner or Notary Public
  • Intend to keep living in Ireland, attend a ceremony and be of good character

3. Refugees

  • 18 or over with a declaration as a refugee from the Minister for Justice
  • You can apply 5 years from the exact date you were granted international protection
  • Proof of residence for the past five years, with your permission kept registered with no gaps
  • If you don't have a passport, you can prove identity with a certified copy of your Irish travel document, a letter confirming refugee status, and a passport affidavit (with your full name, date of birth, why a passport can't be obtained and how you arrived in Ireland)
  • No certification fee for recognised refugees and stateless people

4. Applying for a child (under 18)

A child can't apply for themselves – a parent, legal guardian or someone acting in loco parentis applies on their behalf.

  • Child of a naturalised parent: the parent is an Irish citizen by naturalisation and the child meets the residence conditions
  • Irish descent or association: the Minister may grant citizenship to a child related by blood or adoption to an Irish citizen (or someone entitled to be one). Decided case by case
  • Born in Ireland on or after 1 January 2005 but not a citizen at birth: 1 year of continuous residence immediately before applying, plus 2 years in the 8 years before that. Residence in Northern Ireland doesn't count for this
  • Documents include the child's passport and birth certificate and original school letters for the 3 years before applying

5. Dependant young adults (18–23)

You can apply as a dependant young adult if you meet the adult requirements and you are aged 18–23, entered Ireland legally as part of a family, are in secondary school here (or went straight from secondary school into third level here), and still depend on your parents financially. A letter from your school or college can be one of your residence proofs.

6. EU, EEA, UK and Swiss nationals

You don't have immigration stamps, so don't use the stamp calculator. Instead you submit documents proving how long you've lived in Ireland. You must have held EU/EEA, UK or Swiss nationality for more than 5 years – otherwise you apply as a non-EU/EEA national.

Documents: the citizenship scorecard

Proof of identity – 150 points (once)

Send one or more documents from ISD's identity scorecard that add up to 150 points. A certified colour copy of the photo page of your valid home-country passport is worth 150 points on its own. Other documents include your IRP card and driving licence. Passports go through anti-fraud checks and ISD can ask for the original.

Proof of residence – 150 points for every year

  • Type A documents are worth 100 points and Type B documents 50 points – send one of each for every year you claim
  • Each document must show your name, home address and a date
  • Examples: employment detail summary (in your own name), Department of Social Protection contribution or payment statement, bank statements showing at least three consecutive months of point-of-sale transactions in Ireland each year, household bills (gas, electricity, phone, bins, TV licence), RTB letters, hospital appointment letters, Local Property Tax or social welfare correspondence
  • Child benefit alone isn't proof of residence
  • Doctors in the HSE or voluntary hospitals can use a Medical Practitioner Employment History Summary
  • All copies must be certified by a practising solicitor, Commissioner for Oaths, Peace Commissioner or Notary Public
If you can't reach 150 points for a year, include an affidavit explaining why. Not reaching the score without one is a common reason applications fail – and the fee isn't refunded.

The certification fee is only paid after your application is approved. Paper applications pay the €175 by bank draft.

How to apply

  1. Check you're eligible – use the calculator to confirm your reckonable days and continuous year.
  2. Gather your proofs – 150 identity points and 150 residence points for each year, all certified.
  3. Complete the online application on ISD's portal and tick the statutory declaration. Spouses of Irish citizens also include their statutory declaration.
  4. Pay the €175 application fee online – your application isn't accepted until it's paid.
  5. Keep your immigration permission valid and registered while you wait, and tell ISD if your address or email changes.

Paper forms (Form 8 for adults, Forms 9–11 for minors) are only available on request through ISD's Customer Service Portal if you can't apply online.

What happens after you apply

ISD says most applications are processed within 12 months. You'll get an application number and a Garda e-vetting link; ISD may ask for more documents. If approved, you pay the certification fee, upload a copy of your IRP (non-EEA nationals) and are invited to a citizenship ceremony. Your certificate arrives by post in the following weeks, and then you can apply for an Irish passport. Track your application →

If your application is refused

The Minister for Justice has absolute discretion. You'll be told the reason, but there's no appeal – you can apply again. Common reasons include not reaching 150 points without an explanatory affidavit and, for paper applications, incomplete forms, missing pages or declarations, and poor-quality copies.
