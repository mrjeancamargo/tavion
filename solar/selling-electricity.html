Selling your solar power to the grid

You get paid for the solar power you don't use. How much each supplier pays, how you get set up and how you're paid.

Rates checked 4 November 2026 · Suppliers can change them at any time

Key facts

  • Under the Clean Export Guarantee (CEG) every supplier must pay you for power you export. They set their own rate – about 15c to 19.5c per kWh in November 2026.
  • You need a smart meter (ESB Networks fits one free) and your installer's NC6 form to ESB Networks.
  • Payment is usually a credit on your bill.
  • Up to €400 a year is tax-free (2024–2028), rising to €600 from 2027 under Budget 2027. Tax rules →
  • A 4 kWp system in Dublin exports about 2,414 kWh a year – about €447 at 18.5c.

Standard rates, not regulated. Some suppliers offer higher rates on special solar plans, often with a higher unit price – compare the whole plan, not just the export rate. Pinergy cut its rate from 25c to 18.5c on 1 August 2026.

How to start getting paid

  1. Your installer submits the NC6 to ESB Networks (systems up to 6 kW single-phase or 11 kW three-phase). Larger systems use the NC7.
  2. ESB Networks fits or sets up a smart meter that measures exports in 30-minute blocks.
  3. ESB tells your supplier export metering is live.
  4. Your supplier starts paying – check your bill shows an export credit. If not after a few months, call them.

Sell or use it yourself?

Every kWh you use yourself saves about 33c; every kWh you sell earns about 18.5c. So run the washing machine, dishwasher and immersion when the sun's out, or add a battery.
